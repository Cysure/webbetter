GEO, AI SEARCH · 5 MIN READ

GEO is the new SEO: how brands get cited by AI assistants

Rankings are no longer the finish line. What large language models read, trust and quote, and how to structure your site so you are the source.

WEB BETTER RESEARCHPublished 12 OCTOBER, 2026

On this page

  1. Where AI answers come from
  2. What the research measured
  3. Write for extraction
  4. Make your brand easy to recognise
  5. A short GEO checklist

Key takeaways

  • AI assistants answer from pages they can reach and index. If the right crawlers are blocked, or Google cannot show a snippet of the page, you are not in the race.
  • In the research that coined the term, adding quotations, statistics and cited sources made content up to 40% more visible in AI answers. Keyword stuffing made it less visible.
  • GEO does not replace SEO. It sits on top of it: Google says AI Overviews draw on pages that are already indexed and eligible for a snippet.

For twenty years the goal of search was simple to describe: rank on page one, ideally in the top three. That goal has not disappeared, but it is no longer the whole story. A growing share of questions now get answered before anyone sees a list of links, by Google's AI Overviews, by ChatGPT, by Perplexity, by Claude. The answer names a handful of sources. Being one of them is the new page one.

That is what generative engine optimisation, or GEO, is about. The term is younger than most of the tools that now use it, and a lot of what gets sold under the label is guesswork. So this piece sticks to what can be checked: what the platforms themselves document, and what the one serious study on the subject actually measured.

Where AI answers come from

Assistants do not invent their sources at random. When they search the web they work from an index, and they fetch pages with their own crawlers. Each platform documents this differently, and the details matter.

  • Google. In its guidance on AI features and your website, Google states that to appear as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode, a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet, and that there are no additional technical requirements.
  • ChatGPT. OpenAI launched ChatGPT search on 31 October 2024 and opened it to everyone in February 2025. Its crawler documentation is explicit: sites that opt out of OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers. GPTBot is a separate crawler used for model training.
  • Claude. Anthropic lists three robots: ClaudeBot for training data, Claude-User when a person asks Claude to look something up, and Claude-SearchBot to improve search results. Brave Search has appeared on Anthropic's subprocessor list since March 2025, and independent testing found Claude's citations matching Brave's results.

The practical consequence is unglamorous but decisive. Check your robots.txt, your firewall and your CDN's bot protection. We regularly see sites that block AI crawlers by default without anyone having decided to. Blocking a training crawler such as GPTBot can be a legitimate choice. Blocking the search crawlers means opting out of the answers.

What the research measured

The term GEO comes from a 2023 paper by Pranjal Aggarwal and colleagues, published at KDD 2024. The team built a benchmark of 10,000 queries, rewrote source pages in different ways and measured how much of each page made it into generated answers.

Three changes did best: adding quotations from credible people, adding statistics, and citing sources. Making the text more fluent also helped. Keyword stuffing, the oldest trick in SEO, scored below the unedited baseline. The effects also varied by topic, which the authors flag as a reason to avoid one-size-fits-all advice.

The most interesting finding is about who benefits. Pages that ranked lower in conventional results gained far more than the top result. Citing sources lifted the visibility of fifth-ranked pages by 115% on average, while the top-ranked page lost ground. For smaller companies competing with large brands, that is the most encouraging line in the study.

One caution: the study measured visibility in a research setup using generative models of the time, not inside the live ChatGPT or Google products. It tells you what kinds of content language models prefer to use. It does not guarantee a citation tomorrow.

Write for extraction

Taken together, the documentation and the research point in the same direction. An assistant has to find your page, understand it quickly and lift a passage that stands on its own. Content that does well tends to share a few traits.

  • The answer comes first. Put the direct answer in the opening sentence of a section, then the nuance. A paragraph that starts with background is hard to quote.
  • Facts are specific. Prices, timelines, numbers and named examples beat adjectives. "Most projects launch in six to ten weeks" can be quoted. "We work fast" cannot.
  • Claims carry their evidence. Link the source, name the study, quote the expert. That is exactly what the GEO experiments rewarded.
  • Each section stands alone. Headings that are real questions, and paragraphs that make sense out of context, survive being cut out of the page.
  • The important text is real text. Facts locked in images, sliders or scripts that load after interaction are easy for people to miss and harder for machines to read.

Make your brand easy to recognise

Assistants also need to understand who you are. Use the same company name, description and contact details on your site, your Google Business Profile, LinkedIn and the directories you appear in. Add structured data (Organization, Service, FAQPage) so that facts like your location, services and languages are machine-readable rather than implied.

You may have heard of llms.txt, a plain-text summary of a site proposed in September 2024. It is cheap to add and we publish one, but be clear about its status: it is a proposal, not a standard, and Google's John Mueller has said publicly that no AI system currently uses it. Treat it as a bonus, never as the strategy.

A short GEO checklist

  1. Allow Googlebot, Bingbot, OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot and PerplexityBot in robots.txt, and check that your CDN does not challenge them.
  2. Make sure your key pages are indexed, with no noindex or nosnippet tags hiding them.
  3. Rewrite your most important pages answer-first, with specific facts, numbers and linked sources.
  4. Add an FAQ that answers the questions customers really ask, in their words.
  5. Keep your company facts identical everywhere they appear, and mark them up with schema.org.
  6. Ask the assistants yourself. Track the questions that matter to your business, monthly, and note who gets cited.

None of this is magic, and none of it is wasted if AI search changes again next year. Pages that are fast, clear, specific and well sourced have always been what search engines tried to find. The difference now is that the reward is not just a click. It is being the answer.
